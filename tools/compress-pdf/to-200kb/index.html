Why your document PDF gets rejected

A scanned certificate or a PDF exported from a phone app is commonly 1–5 MB. Against a 200 KB limit that is 5 to 25 times too large, and re-saving or printing-to-PDF rarely fixes it, because the pages keep their full-resolution images. Fitting under 200 KB requires re-encoding the pages at a size and quality chosen for the limit — exactly what this tool's target search does, at the highest quality that still fits, so the officer verifying your documents can still read them. And if your PDF is already under 200 KB, the tool returns it unchanged and says so — there is nothing to fix.

How to use

  1. Add your PDF — a certificate scan, CNIC PDF, degree or a combined document file, up to 50 pages. The Under 200 KB target is already set.
  2. Press Compress PDF to 200 KB. Every page is rendered at 200 DPI and rebuilt at the highest quality that keeps the whole file at or under 204,800 bytes.
  3. Download and upload. The result panel shows the page count, original size, new size and percentage saved, ticked against your 200 KB limit.

Tips for a PDF that verifies cleanly

  • One document per file, in order. Portals verify fastest when pages follow the order the form asks for.
  • Scan straight and flat, in even light — a skewed, shadowed page causes more rejections than file size ever does, and compression cannot straighten it.
  • Keep your original safe. Compressed pages are images: they read perfectly but text is not selectable. Your original stays your master copy.
  • Different limit? If your portal says 100 KB or 500 KB instead, just switch the target above — or use the main Compress PDF to KB tool with a custom value.

Frequently asked questions

How does the tool guarantee the PDF stays under 200KB?

It renders every page, rebuilds the PDF, measures it, and steps quality and page size down only as far as needed until the whole file is at or under 200 KB (204,800 bytes) — always keeping the highest quality that fits. The result panel shows the true final size. If even the smallest settings cannot reach 200 KB (rare — usually a very long, photo-heavy PDF), the panel says so honestly and suggests splitting the PDF in two.

What if my PDF is already under 200KB?

Then nothing is compressed. The tool detects that your file already fits and returns the original unchanged at full quality, telling you plainly that no compression was needed. Recompressing a file that already fits would only cost quality for nothing.

Will my documents still be readable at under 200KB?

Yes, for portal purposes. Pages are rendered at 200 DPI and quality is reduced only as far as needed to fit 200 KB — the search keeps the highest quality that fits. Certificates, CNIC copies and scanned documents stay clearly readable at the sizes portals display and verify. Photo-heavy pages will look softer; the result panel always shows the exact final size so you can judge before uploading.

Will the text still be selectable after compression?

No — to reach an exact size like 200 KB, each page is rebuilt as a high-quality image, so the pages look the same and stay readable but text cannot be selected or copied, and links or form fields do not carry over. Portals only view and verify pages, so for uploads this is exactly what is needed. Keep your original as the editable master copy.

Which portals ask for a PDF under 200KB?

Many government job, admission and document-verification portals in Pakistan and India cap document PDFs at 200 KB — it is the most common document-upload limit, with 100 KB, 300 KB and 500 KB also seen. This page is pre-set to 200 KB; if your portal states a different limit, switch the target above or use the main Compress PDF to KB tool with any custom value.

Is my PDF uploaded to your server?

No. Compression happens inside your browser on your own device using PDF.js (an open-source reader that downloads once from a public CDN — a program file, not your document). Your PDF is never sent anywhere, never stored and never seen by anyone.
