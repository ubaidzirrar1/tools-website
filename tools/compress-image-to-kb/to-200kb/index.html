Why compress an image to exactly 200KB?

Two hundred kilobytes is the limit behind one of the biggest application rushes in the world: Indonesia's CPNS government-job registration requires a pas foto of maximum 200 KB in JPG format, and millions of applicants meet that rule every cycle. The same generous cap appears on document-photo uploads, some university and visa portals, and anywhere the reviewer genuinely needs to see detail in your photo.

At 200 KB you are not starving the image — a well-compressed 200 KB photo looks close to the original on screen. The tool still works backwards from the cap, because a percentage shrink of a 5 MB phone photo can leave you at 400 KB and rejected. It finds the highest quality that fits under 200 KB, touching dimensions only for extremely large originals.

Who needs a 200KB image?

  • CPNS / SSCASN pas foto — maximum 200 KB, JPG (Indonesia)
  • KTP and document photos for Indonesian registrations
  • Detailed photos for portals allowing up to 200 KB
  • Higher-quality form photos wherever the stated cap allows them

Frequently asked questions

Is 200KB really the CPNS pas foto limit?

Yes — SSCASN registration has consistently required a pas foto in JPG at a maximum of 200 KB, usually with a red background and formal dress. This tool handles the file size and format; the background and dress are about the photo itself.

Will text stay readable if I compress a document photo to 200KB?

Generally yes — 200 KB is a comfortable budget for one photographed page, certificate or ID card, and text stays legible when the photo is taken straight-on with even light. The result panel shows the exact final size before you download.

My form says “maximum 200KB”. What exactly does this tool produce?

It targets at or just under 200 KB, never above it, so the file passes a maximum-200 KB check. If you prefer a safety margin, set the target box to 195 KB — the visual difference is nil.
