How to get a good signature scan

The resize is the easy part — most failed signature uploads were lost at the photograph. Four habits make yours pass first time:

  • Dark pen, plain white paper. Sign with a dark blue or black pen on a clean, unlined sheet. Ruled lines get baked into the image and look like part of the signature.
  • Sign a little bigger than usual. Your signature will be reduced to 140 × 60 pixels, so bold, slightly larger strokes survive the shrink; thin, faint ones can break up or vanish.
  • Good light, no shadow. Photograph in bright, even light — daylight near a window is ideal — and keep your phone and your own shadow off the paper. A shadow turns half the background grey, the classic cause of “unclear signature” rejections.
  • Crop tightly. In the tool, frame just the signature with a small white margin. The tighter the crop, the larger and clearer the strokes are in the final 140 × 60 file.

Why signatures get rejected

A signature upload has to pass the same three software checks as a photo — JPG format, a file size inside the stated limits, and the exact pixel dimensions — and it fails them in its own particular ways. The dimensions catch people out first: 140 × 60 is a wide, short strip, while a phone photo is a tall rectangle, so a signature that has simply been “compressed” still has completely the wrong shape and is refused no matter how small the file is. That is why this tool crops to the shape first and only then worries about kilobytes.

The size limits cut in both directions. Shrink a signature too enthusiastically and heavy JPEG compression smears the strokes into grey mush — and a signature that no longer matches the one you sign on the exam-day attendance sheet can cause trouble at verification, long after the upload was accepted. Compress too little, or photograph in such detail that the file stays heavy, and you break the ceiling. And as the next section explains, some bands punish you for making the file too small as well. Add the everyday faults — a shadow across the paper, a bluish or grey background instead of white, strokes so pale they fade at small sizes — and it is easy to see why the signature upload, the smallest file on the form, causes a disproportionate share of rejected applications.

The KB band has a floor, not just a ceiling

SSC and IBPS do not ask for “under 20 KB”; they ask for a signature between 10 KB and 20 KB. A clean signature — dark strokes, white paper, nothing else in the frame — is a very simple image, and at 140 × 60 pixels it commonly encodes to just 4–7 KB at the highest JPEG quality. That file is, paradoxically, too good: the portal rejects it for falling below the minimum. When that happens here, the tool appends empty JPEG comment segments — a legal, invisible part of the file format that viewers and decoders simply skip — until the file size rises into the band. The picture is untouched; the file just carries a little ballast, and the result panel tells you plainly when this has been done.

Frequently asked questions

What size should my signature be for online forms?

For SSC and IBPS the published specification is 140 × 60 pixels, between 10 KB and 20 KB, as a JPG — both presets are built into the tool above. Other exams and portals set their own numbers, and many simply ask for a signature under 10 KB or under 50 KB; the tool has presets for those too, plus a Custom preset where you can type any pixel size and KB limit from your notification.

How do I resize my signature to 20KB?

Upload the photo of your signature, crop it with the locked 140 × 60 frame and choose the SSC Signature or IBPS Signature preset, then press Make my signature. Portals that say “20KB” almost always mean the 10–20 KB band, and the tool lands your file inside it: it lowers JPEG quality if the file is too big, and if the picture alone comes out below 10 KB it pads the file invisibly to reach the band — the signature itself is never altered. There is also a dedicated resize signature to 20KB page with the preset already selected.

Why was my signature rejected for being too small?

Because bands like SSC's and IBPS's 10–20 KB have a minimum as well as a maximum, and a clean signature on white paper often encodes to only 4–7 KB at 140 × 60 pixels. The portal's software rejects it just as firmly as an oversized file. This tool detects the shortfall and adds harmless padding data inside the JPEG (comment segments that decoders skip) until the size enters the band.

My form only says “signature under 10 KB” and gives no pixel size. What should I use?

Use the Under 10 KB preset (or the dedicated resize signature to 10KB page). It keeps the widely accepted 140 × 60 pixel dimensions and guarantees the file stays at or below 10 KB. If your notification does list different pixels, choose Custom and type them in — the crop frame locks to whatever shape you enter.

Is my signature uploaded to your server?

No. Cropping, resizing and encoding all run inside your browser on your own device. Your signature is never sent anywhere, never stored and never seen by anyone — sensible, since a signature is a security feature of your identity documents.
