Why portals reject your document PDF

A phone photograph of a certificate is commonly 3–5 MB. Two of them in a PDF is 6–10 MB — against a 200 KB limit, that is 30 to 50 times too large, and no amount of re-saving fixes it, because a PDF made by simply wrapping full-size photos inherits their size. Fitting under 200 KB requires re-encoding the images at a size and quality chosen for the limit — exactly what this tool's target search does, at the highest quality that still fits, so your documents stay readable for the officer who verifies them.

How to use

  1. Add your images — CNIC front and back, certificates, degrees — up to 20. The Under 200 KB target is already set.
  2. Arrange the pages with the ↑ ↓ buttons (front before back, degrees in order). A4 with a small margin suits documents.
  3. Create and download. The result panel shows the page count and exact byte size, ticked against your 200 KB limit — then upload with confidence.

Tips for document photos that verify cleanly

  • Photograph documents flat and straight, filling the frame, in bright even light — a skewed, shadowed CNIC causes more rejections than file size ever does.
  • One document per photo. Cropping two documents out of one photo halves the readable detail of each.
  • Check the order before creating — portals verify front-then-back and in-order certificates fastest.
  • Different limit? If your portal says 100 KB or 500 KB instead, just switch the target above — or use the main Image to PDF converter with a custom value.

Frequently asked questions

How does the tool guarantee the PDF stays under 200KB?

It builds the PDF, measures it, and re-encodes the images at a lower quality if the total is over 200 KB — repeating the search until the PDF fits or the lowest sensible quality is reached. The result panel always shows the true final size. In the rare case that even the lowest quality cannot fit (usually a very large set of photos), the panel warns you honestly and tells you the smallest size achieved, so you can remove an image and try again.

Will my documents still be readable at under 200KB?

Yes, for portal purposes. The tool reduces image quality only as far as needed to fit the limit, and documents are mostly flat colour and text, which compress very efficiently. Text photographed reasonably straight and in focus stays clearly readable at the size portals display and verify documents.

Which portals ask for a PDF under 200KB?

Many government job, admission and document-verification portals in Pakistan and India cap document PDFs at 200 KB — it is the most common document-upload limit, with 100 KB, 300 KB and 500 KB also seen. This page is pre-set to 200 KB; if your portal states a different limit, switch the size target on this page (100 / 500 KB or a custom value) or use the main Image to PDF converter.

Can I put my CNIC front and back in one PDF?

Yes — that is one of the most common uses. Add the front photo first and the back photo second (use the arrow buttons to fix the order if needed), keep A4 and Under 200 KB, and create the PDF. Each photo becomes one page. Keep the photos straight, fully inside the frame, and taken in even light so all details stay verifiable.

Are my documents uploaded to your server?

No. The PDF is built inside your browser on your own device. Your documents are never sent anywhere, never stored and never seen by anyone — which is exactly how CNICs and certificates should be handled.
