Made for portal document uploads

Most image-to-PDF converters are built for sharing photos. This one is built for the uploads that decide applications: a CNIC front and back that must become one PDF under a portal's size cap, degree and certificate scans for a job application, a set of documents a university portal wants as a single file. That is why the size target is a first-class option rather than an afterthought — set it to your portal's limit and the tool finds the highest quality that fits, then shows you the exact byte count so you can be sure before you upload. Need the target pre-set? The dedicated Image to PDF Under 200KB page starts with 200 KB already chosen.

How to use

  1. Add your images. Drag & drop them, tap to select, or paste — up to 20, each becoming one page.
  2. Arrange and set options. Use ↑ and ↓ to order the pages; pick A4 for documents (the safe default), a margin, and a size target if your portal sets one.
  3. Create and download. The result panel reports the page count and exact size — and warns you honestly if a target could not be met, instead of quietly handing you an oversized file.

Which settings should I use?

  • Certificates, CNIC, degrees for a portal: A4, small margin, and the portal's KB limit as the target (200 KB is the most common).
  • A set of photos to send someone: Fit to image, no margin, best quality.
  • Documents for email: A4, small margin, Under 500 KB keeps the attachment light while staying sharp.
  • Printing at a shop: A4, small margin, best quality — the file prints exactly like a normal document.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a PDF under 200KB for a form upload?

Add your images, choose “Under 200 KB” as the PDF size target, and press Create PDF. The tool re-encodes your images at the highest quality that keeps the whole PDF at or under 200 KB (204,800 bytes) and shows you the exact final size. If even the lowest quality cannot fit — usually with many large photos — the result panel says so plainly instead of pretending. There is also a dedicated Image to PDF Under 200KB page with the target already set.

What page size should I choose — A4 or Fit to image?

A4 for documents: certificates, CNIC copies, degrees and anything a portal or office will print — every image is placed on a standard A4 page. Fit to image makes each PDF page exactly the shape of its image, which suits photo-only PDFs. Letter is the North American equivalent of A4.

Can I change the order of pages in the PDF?

Yes. Every image you add appears in a list with up and down buttons — arrange them in the order you want the pages to appear, and remove any image with the ✕ button. The PDF is built in exactly the order shown.

Will the quality of my documents stay readable?

With no size target, images are embedded at high quality (up to 2000 pixels on the long edge), which keeps text in photographed documents sharp. With a KB target, quality is traded for size only as much as needed to fit — and for document uploads to portals, the result remains perfectly readable at the sizes portals display.

Are my images uploaded to your server?

No. The PDF is built inside your browser on your own device — the converter is written in JavaScript and your images are never sent anywhere, never stored and never seen by anyone. That matters when the images are personal documents like CNICs and certificates.

How many images can I put in one PDF?

Up to 20 images per PDF, each becoming one page. For a larger set, make two PDFs — or check whether the portal actually wants a single file, since many accept a few separate uploads.
