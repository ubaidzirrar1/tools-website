What the NTA bulletin asks for — and what it doesn't

The figures on this page come from the official NTA JEE (Main) 2027 information bulletin, read directly — not from coaching sites. For the photograph it asks for a recent colour passport-size photo, JPG/JPEG, between 10 KB and 200 KB, with a white background, your face filling about 80% of the frame including your ears, and no mask or cap covering the face. For the signature it asks for JPG/JPEG, between 10 KB and 100 KB, clearly legible.

One honest caution: the bulletin fixes the format, the KB ranges and the photo rules only — it does not state any centimetre or pixel dimensions. The sizes quoted everywhere else — typically 3.5 × 4.5 cm for the photo and 3.5 × 1.5 cm for the signature — are the standard passport-size convention, not NTA figures. This tool's presets simply render at those conventional sizes at 300 DPI (413 × 531 px photo, 413 × 177 px signature) and fit the file inside the official KB band, which is the part NTA actually checks. And a second caution: some older guides quote a much smaller signature range than 10–100 KB. That figure is not in the 2027 bulletin and is out of date — the bulletin range is the one to follow. If the form in front of you ever shows different figures, your form wins — use the Custom size option and enter exactly what it asks for.

Frequently asked questions

What is the photo size for JEE Main?

The official NTA JEE (Main) 2027 information bulletin asks for a recent colour passport-size photograph in JPG/JPEG format, between 10 KB and 200 KB, with a white background and your face filling about 80% of the frame, ears visible, and no mask or cap covering the face. The bulletin does not state any centimetre or pixel dimensions — the size you will see quoted elsewhere, typically 3.5 × 4.5 cm, is the standard passport-size convention, not an NTA figure. This tool's preset renders the photo at 413 × 531 pixels (3.5 × 4.5 cm at 300 DPI) and fits the file inside the official 10–200 KB band.

What is the signature size for JEE Main?

JPG/JPEG format, between 10 KB and 100 KB, and clearly legible — that is what the JEE (Main) 2027 bulletin states. One warning: some older guides quote a much smaller signature range. That figure is not in the 2027 bulletin and is out of date — use 10–100 KB. The bulletin gives no dimensions for the signature either; typically 3.5 × 1.5 cm is quoted by convention, and this tool's preset uses 413 × 177 pixels (3.5 × 1.5 cm at 300 DPI) fitted into the 10–100 KB band.

Why is my JEE Main photo being rejected? What should I check?

Four things, in order: (1) the file size is inside 10–200 KB — below 10 KB is rejected just as surely as above 200 KB, and this tool pads small files up into the band for exactly that reason; (2) the format is JPG/JPEG, not PNG or a phone screenshot; (3) the photograph itself follows the bulletin — recent, colour, white background, face about 80% of the frame with ears visible, no mask, cap or sunglasses hiding the face; (4) you uploaded the photo to the photo field and the signature to the signature field. The result panel on this page shows the exact pixels and bytes of the file you downloaded, so you can compare it line-by-line with what the form asks for.

Does JEE Main need my name and date printed on the photo?

No. JEE Main does not ask for your name or the date printed on the photograph — that requirement belongs to some other exams (SSC and NEET ask for it). Upload a plain, recent passport-size photo that meets the bulletin rules above. If you are applying to one of those other exams as well, our Add Name & Date on Photo tool prepares that version separately.

The JEE form also asks for a live photo. What is that — can this tool make it?

While you fill in the application form, it also captures a live photo of you on the spot — through your webcam, or through a QR code you scan with your phone. That live capture is a separate step inside the form, and it is not a replacement for the scanned photograph you upload: the form wants both. This tool prepares the scanned photo (and signature) that you upload as files; the live photo can only be taken inside the form itself, at the time you fill it.

Is my photo uploaded to your server?

No. The resizing happens inside your browser on your own device. Your photo and signature are never sent anywhere, never stored and never seen by anyone — the sensible way to handle identity documents.
