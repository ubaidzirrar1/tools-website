Resizing a photo for FPSC online apply

Applications on the FPSC portal (fpsc.gov.pk) are completed online, and the photograph upload is one of the steps candidates most often get stuck on: the portal accepts only a small JPG, and an oversized camera photo — easily 3–6 MB from a modern phone — is refused outright. The tool above is already set to the FPSC photo preset, so the whole job is: add your photo, drag the 200 × 230 frame over your face, press Make my photo, and download the finished file.

FPSC advertisements have typically asked for a passport-style photograph as a small JPG, commonly under 30 KB. Treat that figure with a little care: the commission writes the exact requirement in each job advertisement, and the advertisement — not any website, including this one — is the final authority. Before your closing date, read the “instructions to candidates” in your ad once more. If it states a different KB limit than the preset, switch the tool to Custom and type those numbers; the crop frame and the band-fitting work exactly the same way.

Two habits save repeat attempts. First, start from a recent, front-facing photo against a plain, light background — FPSC compares it with you in the test centre. Second, keep the downloaded file unchanged once the tool makes it: renaming it is harmless, but re-saving it in a gallery app or sending it through a chat app can re-encode it to a different size and break the very limit you just fixed.

Frequently asked questions

What size photo does FPSC require?

FPSC advertisements have typically asked for a passport-style JPG photograph under about 30 KB, and this page's preset produces a 200 × 230 px JPG under that limit. The exact requirement is printed in each job advertisement, so confirm it there — if your ad gives a different limit, use the tool's Custom preset with those numbers.

The FPSC portal rejected my photo even though it was small. Why?

Size is only part of the check — the file must also be a JPG and, on most portals, sensible photo dimensions. This tool outputs exactly 200 × 230 px as a JPG under the KB limit, covering all three. Also make sure you are uploading the downloaded file itself, not a screenshot of it.

Is my photo uploaded to your server?

No. The resizing runs entirely in your browser on your own device — your photo is never sent to a server or stored anywhere.
